Roadmap

Boxes are epics; arrows are what waits for what. Descriptions are in the table below, by number. Every test is the full run (drive, park, reach, stop) of the two networks together, no expert in the loop, on the same 100 test dots.

What each epic is

#EpicWhat it is, what it needs, what it produces
CORE
1Golden expert dataThe one dataset everything is measured against. Clean expert runs: no effects, the fixed start pose, all 1 ms rows. 100 training dots chosen from the 800 so bearing, distance and height are each spread evenly; the existing 100 validation dots and 100 test dots. Already collected; the training selection is in dots_spread.json.
2Camera renderThe corner camera rendered every 1/30 s along the 100 golden training runs and the 100 validation runs (the validation loss needs camera inputs too), start to stop, with the dot detections saved. The two trackers (driving: distance and bearing; reaching: x, y, height) turn them into the camera inputs each network trains on. About 40 minutes for 200 runs.
3The two networks The architectures of iterations 13 and 27, unchanged, retrained on the golden data and run together end to end: the driving network drives and parks, and its own PARK output hands over to the reaching network.

Driving (iteration 13), 30 Hz. Inputs 16: 4 wheel-encoder angles, 4 wheel-encoder speeds, their mean angle, right minus left angle, mean speed, right minus left speed; camera dot distance, sin and cos of its bearing, visible flag. 16 → 16 ReLU → 16 ReLU → 4 wheel speeds and time to park. The time-to-park head predicts a value in 0 to 1 that is multiplied by 3 s; PARK when that time is 0.25 s or less (camera_loop.py:71). The six angle inputs standardised with the training mean and standard deviation, folded into the first layer. Rows: start to PARK plus 100 parked rows per run (wheels 0, time to park 0). Adam lr 0.01, full batch.

Reaching (iteration 27), 1 kHz. Inputs 17: 7 arm-encoder angles, 7 arm-encoder speeds, camera dot x, y, height (height-keeping tracker, held between frames). Torque network 17 → 64 ReLU → 64 ReLU → 7 torques. Stop network, separate, 17 → 32 ReLU → 16 ReLU → 1, the distance to the dot clipped at 0.25 m; stop when the prediction ≤ 10 mm. Angles divided by π, speeds by each joint's limit, dot by 1 m; torques standardised per joint; both folded into the weights. Rows: PARK to the expert's stop. Adam lr 0.01, full batch.

Seeds: 7, 1 and 2 for every network; the run with no noise is reported for all three with its spread, and the median seed is used for everything below. Any difference between conditions smaller than the seed spread is not a finding.
Epochs: every network up to 300, the count chosen on the 100 validation dots at the lowest validation loss (the stop network's up to 600, as in iteration 27). Saves: driving_seedN.npz, reaching_seedN.npz (torque + stop) and a loss history per epoch for each output.
4Run with no noiseThe full-run harness and the headline number in one. The harness (full_run.py, working): both networks, no expert, inputs from the camera and encoders only, any list of dots, runs in parallel, logs the state every 1/30 s; every effect and lens below is a switch in it. The result: the 100 test dots with nothing switched on; success rate (gripper within 10 mm of the dot at stop) with its ±10-point interval, median closest, where it parked against where the expert parked, for the three seeds. If it fails, the handover is checked first, then the reach, before anything below runs.
ROBUSTNESS LADDER (tests only; networks from 3 fixed; each step keeps the ones before; doses from the course's settings, not tuned; one curve of success against effects on)
5+ encoder noise Where it enters: in the measurement pipeline, before the speed estimate. Gaussian noise with the course's standard deviation (0.0001 rad, joint_cov) is added to the true joint angle, the encoder then counts that noisy angle in whole ticks, and the speed is the difference of counts over the sampling time, as now. Angle and speed are therefore perturbed consistently; perturbing them independently (the course does that to the expert's measurements) would be a different experiment. Expected: no detectable change, because 0.0001 rad is below one wheel tick (0.0015 rad) and the arm's ticks (about 2 × 10⁻⁶ rad) are far below the network's resolution. A flat step is a result.
6+ damping The course's motor model (servo_motor.py, compute_torque): an extra torque is added to every motor's command, τextra = M(q) · (−c · q̇ − k · q), where M(q) is the joint-space mass matrix (kg m²), q and q̇ the measured joint angles (rad) and speeds (rad/s) of all joints, and the product is taken over all 17 joints before the 11 motors' components are applied. The collector implements exactly this (M.bottomRightCorner(17,17) * (−c·q̇ − k·q) on the measured state; parity with the Python simulator checked). Because the term goes through the mass matrix, c has units 1/s and k has units 1/s²; the torque is in N m. Affected joints: the 4 wheels and the 7 arm joints (the fingers have no motor).
Damping: c = 1.0 (1/s), k = 0. A viscous drag proportional to each joint's speed, scaled by the inertia it sees.
7+ elasticityElasticity: k = 1.0 (1/s²) in the same equation: a spring pulling every joint towards angle zero, scaled by the mass matrix. Note that the wheels are continuous joints whose angle grows as they turn, so this spring on the wheels grows with distance driven; the expert still reached 997 of 1,000 dots under it (median run 8.6 s), so the effect at k = 1.0 is small. The course gives no value; 1.0 is ours.
8+ inertiaInertia: commented out in the course code; the commented line is τextra += −J · q̈prev with J = motor_inertia_coeff and q̈prev the previous step's measured acceleration, not through the mass matrix. We switch it on with J = 0.001 kg m² (about half the smallest joint inertia, 0.0021 kg m², so the one-step lag stays stable). Caveat for the write-up: this is an acceleration-dependent torque correction with a one-step lag, not a physical rotor inertia added to the links; it approximates one for slow changes of acceleration only.
ADVERSARIAL LENSES (on the run with no noise, not on the ladder)
9Delay 3 msThe networks' inputs delayed by three simulator steps, the course's value, which breaks the expert. Test-time only. (1 ms as a second point if time.)
9Random start pose Is it possible to collect? Yes. The expert navigates in world coordinates (base position and target from the simulator), so it drives correctly from any start pose; only the networks are restricted to relative inputs. The collector resets the robot by reloading a saved state; setting a start pose after that is one call through the pose command API already bound in bullet_api.hpp (base position and orientation). Small change.
Data: the same 100 training dots and 100 test dots, each with one random start pose: position uniform in ±0.5 m, heading uniform over 360°. New expert runs are needed because the expert's path from a new start is a new run. 200 runs, minutes to collect, plus 20 minutes to render the training half. The dots are reused: a new start with the same dot is a new relative geometry, which is all the networks see.
Tested two ways: the golden networks from random starts (if they lose accuracy, something depends on where the run begins; by design nothing should), and the pair retrained on the random-start runs. The retrained pair goes into the stack.
9Imperfect expert record The expert is remote; its log reaches us over a link that drops bursts. The physics is untouched; rows of the golden record are removed before training. Training-time only: retrain per case, test clean.
Literature check: incomplete demonstrations are a studied setting. Sun and Ma (IJCAI 2019) learn from demonstrations where actions are missing for part of the states; TAILO (offline imitation from incomplete trajectories) weights the cloning loss along the trajectory to cope with missing steps. Both use RL-style machinery; for plain behaviour cloning the gap simply means the network never sees those states, which is the effect we want to measure, so the setting is sound. Two consequences shape the design: (a) a cut of the last 0.5 s before the stop removes every row with a small distance target, so the stop network may never predict ≤ 10 mm and the run ends at the time cap; that is the expected finding, not a bug, and the harness's cap handles it; (b) at 1 kHz, random drops of single rows change nothing, so only bursts are informative.
Cases: three targeted cuts (0.25 s either side of PARK; the last 0.5 s before the stop; the first 0.5 s of driving), then random bursts of 0.25 to 1 s at 10% and 30% lost if time. Each case is a retrained pair and its own test.
9Goal occlusionA visual-only sheet, 0.3 × 0.2 m, moves at 0.1 m/s across the camera's line to the dot from 0.5 s after PARK, hiding it for about 2 s and clearing with 2 s left. A collision is counted if the hand crosses the sheet's plane inside its outline. Test-time only; tests the tracker's held estimate and the stop.
9Full adversarial stackAll four at once: the pair trained on the random-start runs with the record cut around PARK (the harshest cut), tested from random start poses with the 3 ms delay and the occluder. The worst case, and the one combination that matters most. Of the pairs, only delay + occlusion gets its own test: both attack the tracker's memory of the dot, so they may interact; the other pairs touch different phases of the run and should add up.
IMPROVEMENT LENSES (new data, retrain, each tested on the run with no noise and on the full stack)
9DARTExpert runs collected with noise on the actions sent to the motors and the clean actions as labels, so the expert shows recoveries from off-path states. 100 training dots × 2 noise seeds, noise level checked on 10 dots first for the expert's success rate. Retrain as in 3.
9DAggerRoll out the pair, record the states it visits, ask the expert for its command in each, add them, retrain; three rounds. Needs a mode in the native code that returns the expert's command for a given state and time since PARK.
9DART + DAggerDAgger rounds started from the DART-trained pair, with the DART runs kept in the data.
FINAL STEP
9Direct weight editingStarting from the best pair above, Fable reads the validation logs and proposes edits to a small, interpretable part (the stop threshold, an input scale, an output bias). Each edit is scored on the validation dots under the full stack, kept or reverted, logged in MLflow. The same number of random edits is the control. The result is tested on the run with no noise and the full stack.

Test count and order

Run with no noise 3 tests (one per seed), ladder 4, lenses 4 alone + 1 stack + 1 pair, record loss 3 more retrained pairs, improvements 3 × 2, weight editing 2: 24 tests of about an hour each, about 24 h of testing in all. Order: 2 and the effects port in parallel; 3; 4; then the ladder and the lenses as one scripted queue; the improvements as their data arrives; 17 last. Cut first if behind: 16, then 15, then the random-burst half of 11, then the delay + occlusion pair.

Extra tests worth an hour, if there is time

Any trained networks can be tested under any switch, so these are the combinations beyond the plan that would tell us something, in order:

  1. DART networks under delay, and under occlusion. DART is the fix for compounding error, and those two conditions are where compounding error bites. 2 tests.
  2. DART networks at the end of the ladder (all motor effects on). Does recovery data also buy robustness to the physics? 1 test.
  3. The stop-cut network (step 11, last 0.5 s removed) under occlusion. A network that never saw the end of the reach, tested when the dot disappears near the end. 1 test.
  4. The random-start-trained networks with no noise. Does training on random starts cost anything on the fixed start? 1 test.
  5. The golden networks under the full stack. How much of the stack's cost the stack-trained networks recover. 1 test.
